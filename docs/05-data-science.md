# 05. Data science

The five models as built: the AVM, the hedonic price index, rental yields, the collateral stress test and the forecast. Full results are in the reports linked from each section; every choice is in the Decisions log (§8).

## 1. Automated valuation model (AVM)

**Goal.** Estimate the market value of a residential unit at its sale date, as a bank does for collateral. Output per sale: the AVM value and `gap = (price − AVM value) / AVM value`.

**Population.** Clean residential market sales (apartments, villas / townhouses), off-plan and ready together with `is_offplan` as a feature, accuracy reported for each. Fitted from **2011**; 2010 is history for trailing features only.

**Split (out of time).** Train 2011–2023, validation 2024 (tuning, champion choice), test 2025-01 to the snapshot (reported numbers). The tuned model is re-fitted on 2011–2024.

**Features and the as-of rule.** Every market feature of a month-M sale uses months before M only: trailing comparable medians (area, project, building, cell), trailing volumes, rolling hedonic OLS prices, and index changes from **real-time index vintages** (the index method re-run on sales before M). `tests/test_avm_features.py` proves it. Projects and buildings enter as trailing medians, not target encodings. Property age and developer are not available (the DLD projects file isn't loaded).

**Models.**
1. **Comparable sales** (baseline): median price per sq m of the same area × type × bedrooms × off-plan cell over the previous 6 months, ≥ 5 comparables.
2. **Index-adjusted comparables:** the cell's last 12 months of sales, each moved by the real-time index change to M−1.
3. **Rolling hedonic OLS:** the type-level time-dummy fit on [M−36, M−1].
4. **LightGBM** (champion): predicts ln(AED per sq m) **relative to an as-of reference price** (trees can't extrapolate levels), Huber loss, native categoricals, 20 Optuna trials on 2024, median-calibrated. Tuned parameters are committed in `artifacts/avm/best_params.json`.

**Evaluation.** MdAPE and ±10% / ±20% hit rates (plus MAPE, R² on log price), on test, overall and by ready / off-plan, type, price band (by predicted value), top 20 areas and month, always against the baselines on the same sales. An MdAPE under 3% or a ±10% hit rate over 90% raises a leakage alarm and nothing is written.

**Results (test, 275,702 sales, 2025-01 → 2026-09).** LightGBM **MdAPE 6.8%, ±10% 65.0%, ±20% 88.2%**, against comparables 9.9% / 50.3% / 76.1%, index-adjusted comparables 10.1% / 49.5% and rolling OLS 12.3% / 41.7%. LightGBM leads in every reported segment except villas, where comparables are slightly better on MdAPE (8.51% vs 8.66% on the villas both can value) while LightGBM leads on ±20% (84.6% vs 81.5%), and three of the top 20 areas. A 2010-start ablation scores 7.54% vs 7.50%. No leakage alarm.

**Explainability and use.** TreeSHAP importance and dependence plots; worked examples chosen by a published rule, including one large miss. Sales more than 25% from their AVM value are flagged for collateral review (out-of-sample rows only, 6.8% of test sales) as statistical anomalies, not accusations.

Outputs: `reports/avm_model_card.md`; `ml.avm_score`, `ml.avm_performance`, `ml.feature_importance`; views `rpt.avm_*`.

## 2. Hedonic price index

- **Method:** time-dummy hedonic regression, `ln(price per sq m) ~ characteristics + area effects + period dummies`, published as a **rolling-window time dummy**: 36-month windows stepped 12 months, chained on the overlapping periods. Starts January 2011; Jan 2019 = 100. Villas use bedroom-known sales only. The pooled 2011–2026 fit is kept as the robustness check.
- **Segments:** Dubai, apartments, villas, and 15 zone × type series; monthly where min-n holds, else quarterly, else not published; a noise gate drops series whose period changes are too erratic.
- **Why hedonic:** raw medians move with the mix of what sells. In 2023 the raw apartment median rose about 1% while like-for-like prices rose about 17% (`reports/price_index.md`).
- **Validation:** YoY of our index averaged over the trailing 12 months vs DLD's YoY: **r = 0.93 Dubai, 0.92 apartments, 0.91 villas** (2012-12 to 2024-05; DLD's file ends in May 2024). Month for month 0.68–0.72, with ours leading DLD by about six months.
- **Risk metrics:** YoY, 12-month volatility, drawdown from the running peak, episodes (falls ≥ 10%). 2014–2020 is one episode: Dubai −24%, trough September 2020.

Outputs: `reports/price_index.md`; `ml.fct_price_index`; view `rpt.price_index`.

## 3. Rental yields

- `gross_yield = median annual rent / median sale price` per area (and zone) × property type × bedrooms × quarter, with **n ≥ 20 on both sides**; zone medians are recomputed from rows, not averaged.
- Sale side: clean **ready** sales. Rent side: market rents (new, single-line contracts, rule C11).
- Cells outside a 2–15% sanity band are flagged, kept in `ml` / `rpt` and left out of the aggregate figures.
- Gross yield ignores service charges, vacancy and fees; every output says so. Project-level cells are not built (too few areas reach min-n).

Outputs: `reports/yields.md`; `ml.agg_yield_quarter`; view `rpt.yield_quarter`.

## 4. Collateral stress test (illustrative)

**Question.** If prices fall X%, what share of recent buyers would be in negative equity, and where?

- **Population:** 459,644 clean residential sales from the last 36 complete months (Sep 2023 – Aug 2026), marked to market with the published index (91% by a zone × type series, 9% by type).
- **Loan bases:** an assumed LTV grid of 50 / 60 / 70 / 80 / 85% (85 = UAE national first-home cap, the worst case); the CBUAE cap in force on each purchase date (expatriate, first home; from `seed_ltv_rules`); and the registered loans of 48,235 matched purchases. Loans are held at origination (no amortisation), which errs on the conservative side.
- **Scenarios:** price shocks 0% to −50% in 5-point steps, and a **2014→2020 historical replay** at two depths: Dubai-wide (−24% for everyone) and each buyer's own series (the upper range). A 2008–11 replay isn't possible because the index starts in 2011; the −40% / −50% steps stand in for it, labelled hypothetical.
- **Off-plan** is never pooled with ready.

**Results.** At 80% LTV, ready apartments: 0% in negative equity at today's values, **22% at −20%**, 73% at −30%; the 2014→2020 replay gives **43% (Dubai-wide) to 80% (own series)**. Ready villas: 0% / 20% / 64%, replay 34% to 64%. Matched loans: median LTV 80% at purchase, 74% today; 23% of apartments and 14% of villas under water at −20%. Off-plan at the 50% cap: 0% at −20%, 30% at −50%.

Every output is labelled **illustrative, not a regulatory stress test**. Outputs: `reports/stress_test.md`; `ml.stress_grid`, `ml.stress_replay`; views `rpt.stress_grid`, `rpt.stress_replay`.

## 5. Market outlook forecast

- **Targets:** the monthly index and monthly market-sales volume, for Dubai, apartments and villas.
- **Models:** SARIMAX with Fed Funds lagged 6 months as the only exogenous driver (EIBOR isn't loaded), orders chosen once by AIC before the first backtest origin; a naive last value as the baseline. Scenarios: rates flat, +100bp, −100bp.
- **Backtest:** 24 rolling origins (Sep 2024 – Aug 2026) on real-time index vintages. SARIMAX **beats naive in 11 of 12 index cells** (Dubai MAPE 1.4% / 2.2% / 4.0% / 5.7% at 1 / 3 / 6 / 12 months vs 1.7 / 2.6 / 4.6 / 7.5%) **but in only 1 of 12 volume cells**. 80% intervals covered 92% of index and 87% of volume outcomes.
- **Central path, rates flat:** Dubai index **+4.6%** to Aug 2027 (80% band −5% to +15%), carrying the 2011–26 drift forward while the latest YoY is +1.0%. The rate coefficient is positive in all six series and significant in none: reported as a limitation.

Outputs: `reports/forecast.md`; `ml.forecast`, `ml.forecast_backtest`; views `rpt.forecast`, `rpt.forecast_backtest`.

## 6. Not built (possible extensions)

- Developer concentration (HHI by developer) and the off-plan handover pipeline, once the DLD projects file is loaded. The report uses a master-project proxy.
- Area segmentation (k-means on yield, growth, volatility, liquidity).
- A 2008–10 index from on-time registrations only (sales registered in the year they were applied for), chained to the 2011+ index, which would restore a 2008–11 stress replay. Risks: a small, selective 2008 sample and year-boundary effects.
